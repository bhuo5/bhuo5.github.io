Wisconsin Racing: 226 Steering Wheel PCB

Steering Wheel redesign w/ ESP32

Redesigned schematic and layout for Steering Wheel control board on the 226 Wisconsin Racing FSAE electric competition car. Reduced size of the PCB by ~50% from previous 223 design iteration by upgrading the MCU, from Raspberry PI CM4 to ESP32 S3, and removing unecessary and outdated systems.

V1 Steering Wheel PCB design iteration

V1 suffered from a flipped display connector footprint on the PCB, preventing use in a full steering wheel assembly, though functional enough to test display and IO ports.

V2 Steering Wheel w/ fixed display connector

V2 added more power indicators and test points for easier debugging, while fixing the flipped display connector footprint and improving the accuracy of the potentiometer's voltage range.

Assembled and mounted steering wheel

Complete steering wheel system used for competition consisting of: a joystick, 2 push buttons, 2 potentiometers, a light bar, LCD display, and 3D printed housing.


Undergraduate Research: Inductive Magnetometer Sensor

Debugging 12V rail on ADC PCB

Discovered the boost converter diode footprint on PCB was flipped, leading to negligible output voltage on the 12V rail. Isolated the issue by completing continuity tests, probing different nets, and comparing PCB circuitry to the working breadboard prototype.

Testing repaired PCB w/ large coil

Tested 2nd prototype magnetometer after assembling a fresh PCB with the correct diode orientation and a new ADC chip. Successfully read the DC magnetic field near the copper coil, sending data over the ESP-NOW protocol to display on a computer using a python script.

Looking inside the Magnetometer

Soldered electrical connections between the amplilfier PCB, LiPo battery cell, charging port, analog-to-digital converter PCB, and optical switch that are mostly housed within the 3D printed sensor coil. Strengthend all connections by adding service loops and hotglue for strain relief.

Charging LiPo cell w/ complete assembly

Designed and iterated 3D printed designs for magnetometer sensor, making optimized assembly decisions and working within tight radial size constraints. Maximized usability by adding a 3D printed spacer within the sensor coil to raise the PCB and allow access to the charging port.
